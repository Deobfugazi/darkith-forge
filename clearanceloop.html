Advance a delivery

One delivery applies the law: anything below its high-water mark ratchets the mark down; anything above it fails unless a recorded waiver is live. Stasis across 5 deliveries is force-degraded to a blocker.

A waiver never raises the high-water mark — it only lets debt exist for a bounded window. When it expires, the debt is a plain regression again.

    Live waivers

    An apoptotic exception: a named, bounded permission for a class to sit above its mark. It expires automatically after its budget. A waiver is never a way to loosen the ratchet.

    How a waiver works

    Granted

    advance("migration spike")
→ JUSTIFIED waiver waiting: 3 → 6
  for 5 deliveries; HWM stays 3

    Expired

    after 5 deliveries
→ EXPIRED waiver waiting
→ verdict FAIL: net addition of debt

    What this is
    
      
    ClearanceLoop measures whether a team's friction is actually clearing. Health is not a tolerance you sit inside; it is a rate. The high-water mark of friction debt may only move down, stasis is scheduled to become fatal, and a rise needs a named, expiring waiver.
    
      
    
        
    switchingcost
    Context switches: leaving and returning to a task.
    
        
    waitingcost
    Blocked time: builds, long tasks, queues.
    
        
    searchingcost
    Hunting: finding the right file, doc or signal.
    
        
    correctingcost
    Rework: errors, failed runs, redone steps.
    
        
    reviewcost
    Review burden: validating output before merge.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    The four laws
    
      
      
        
    1. Monotone clearance — when debt drops, the mark drops with it, and never rises back.
      2. 
        
    2. Regression fails — debt above the mark is a net addition, and the verdict fails.
      3. 
        
    3. Stasis degrades — debt that does not clear across the window is force-degraded to a blocker.
      4. 
        
    4. Apoptotic waiver — a rise is permitted only by a recorded, reasoned, expiring exception.
      5.
