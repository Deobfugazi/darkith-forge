## 0. Scope and safety boundary

This report begins a persistent, source-linked research track for implementing the economic and reciprocal-value mechanisms described by NT XI, NT XII, and NT XIII. It examines open standards, peer-reviewed research, public institutions, and inspectable open-source systems. It separates established mechanisms from hypotheses, authorial synthesis, and unresolved engineering work.

Isolation contract:
- This branch contains research documentation only.
- No production source, CI workflow, workflow trigger, test command, runner, secret, signing key, build configuration, deployment configuration, mainnet endpoint, or current board file is changed by this report.
- No workflow was dispatched by this research action.

## 1. Repository-grounded starting point

### 1.1 Canonical formulary already present

NT XI defines the Autophagic Sovereign Economy (ASE) around:
- W_in: total wealth generated by community labor, care, and resources.
- E: external extraction, including rent, debt interest, algorithmic capture, IP enclosure, and ecological depletion.
- S: locally retained value, including cooperative surplus, community land trust equity, and commons dividends.
- Retention relation: W_in - E = S, with S non-zero and returned to the source.
- A long-run target of reducing E/W_in, subject to increasing well-being and non-increasing resource use.

NT XI also specifies five ASE modules:
1. Subsistence floor / decommodified survival.
2. Dual-stake governance, with worker and community assemblies.
3. Manufactured-scarcity audit.
4. Reciprocal Labor & Care Registry (RLCR), recording care, mentoring, and ecological stewardship and issuing non-transferable credits for community resources.
5. Autophagic Feedback Loop (AFL), with community-set targets and measured feedback.

NT XII describes the mint as the codified harvest of a measured, closed residual and insists on source, transformation, and falsifier at every step. Its preamble also equates tokens with resolved defects and, in one passage, treats the author's expended exhaustion/energy as token backing. That passage must be handled carefully: the expenditure is a recordable cost and provenance fact, but it is not sufficient evidence of a transferable benefit, a market-clearing price, a redeemable claim, or community acceptance.

NT XIII / OFIF supplies a more operational accounting direction:
- named residuals for energy, information, and money;
- dimensional bridges between quantities;
- reclamation and duty;
- high-water-mark ratchets and explicit expiry of tolerated defects;
- a proposed residual-as-bill composition.

This formulary is the design authority to test. The research must not replace it with a generic token or blockchain recipe.

### 1.3 Critical notation collision and dimensional proof obligation

The economic treatises use E for external extraction, while the OFIF formulary also uses E for energy. These are different quantities and must receive distinct canonical identifiers in machine schemas, equations, and code.

OFIF defines:
R_x = x_gen - x_con - d(x_stock)/dt - x_rec - x_wst, for x in {E, I, $}
kappa = E/I, with units joules per bit
rho = $/E, with units dollars per joule
Bill = integral over time of sum_x(R_x(t) * kappa_x * rho_x) dt

Before implementation, the derivation must explicitly define each indexed bridge kappa_x and rho_x, the units of every term in R_x, and prove that every summand has units of currency per time before integration. The currently printed shorthand does not provide enough indexed definitions to infer all three mappings without ambiguity. A residual sum across unlike dimensions is invalid unless every residual is converted through a named, measured bridge. This is not a stylistic concern; it is a type and accounting correctness gate.

## 2. Evidence-backed findings from external research

### 2.1 Offline-first systems are feasible, but offline monetary finality is a separate problem

Android's official offline-first architecture guidance describes local data sources, queued writes, push/pull synchronization, versioning, and explicit conflict resolution. It notes that prolonged offline operation increases conflict and stale-data complexity. Last-write-wins is one possible strategy, not a universal safe financial rule.

CRDT research provides formal convergence mechanisms for replicated data structures. Automerge is an open-source implementation of CRDT data structures and synchronization. CRDTs are suitable candidates for collaborative records, annotations, drafts, and replicated non-financial evidence metadata.

Important distinction: convergent replicas do not, by themselves, prevent two disconnected devices from spending the same scarce monetary balance. Monetary transfers require a single-spend rule, a reservation/escrow mechanism, a bounded offline risk model, or a settlement authority/consensus protocol. A CRDT merge must never silently reconcile conflicting spends by accepting both.

Research consequence:
- Offline contribution capture can be immediate.
- Offline evidence can be signed and queued.
- Offline credits may be provisional, locally bounded, and clearly marked pending.
- Final transferable settlement must wait for a defined double-spend-safe acceptance rule.
- If a design claims unconditional offline spendability and global no-double-spend finality without coordination or bounded risk, that claim is falsified by the partition scenario.

### 2.2 Verifiable credentials and decentralized identifiers are mature standards, not proof of social value

W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 became a Recommendation on 15 May 2025. It provides a standard issuer-holder-verifier model for machine-verifiable claims, with security, privacy, accessibility, and internationalization considerations.

W3C DID Core 1.0 became a Recommendation in 2022. DIDs can be controlled without a central registry, but the standard explicitly does not guarantee that one DID maps permanently to one real-world person. DIDs can be contextual, multiple per controller, and vulnerable to correlation. DID documents should not publish personal data; pairwise identifiers can reduce cross-context correlation.

Research consequence:
- Use signed credentials to attest to skills, roles, service completion, community membership, and evidence review.
- Keep personal data off public ledgers.
- Use pairwise or scoped identifiers by default.
- Do not treat a DID, wallet, device, or account as proof of one unique human.
- Credential trust is contextual: the verifier must evaluate issuer authority, scope, expiry, revocation, and evidence provenance.

### 2.3 Sybil resistance is a social-technical governance problem

The peer-reviewed review “Who Watches the Watchmen?” (Frontiers in Blockchain, 2020) surveys proof-of-personhood systems and identifies the tension between decentralization, privacy, and Sybil resistance. It discusses vouching, voting, interpretation, and physical/social interaction as possible signals, while also identifying collusion, bought identities, coercion, and scalability as unresolved risks.

A newer 2026 Cryptology ePrint paper proposes a cryptographic framework combining personhood credentials and peer-to-peer verifiable relationship credentials with zero-knowledge proofs. It is a research preprint, not a settled global standard.

Research consequence:
- Do not rely on one biometric vendor, government ID, phone number, IP address, wallet, or social graph as the universal personhood oracle.
- Combine independent, context-sensitive evidence and community attestations.
- Apply rate limits and contribution caps while identity assurance is weak.
- Model collusion, coercion, purchased accounts, duplicate enrollment, and exclusion of undocumented or offline populations.
- Use privacy-preserving uniqueness proofs only where proportionate and independently reviewed; do not centralize raw biometrics.

### 2.4 Community and time currencies have promising but weakly quantified impact evidence

A 2020 systematic review of community exchange and time currencies (Public Health, DOI 10.1016/j.puhe.2019.11.011; 38 studies) found consistent qualitative indications of positive effects on social capital and some health/well-being indicators. It also found overall study quality low, few objective outcome measures, no experimental studies, and no studies reporting costs.

Research consequence:
- Community currency is a plausible social mechanism, not a scientifically proven universal solution.
- NT XII must require preregistered outcome measures, comparison groups where feasible, cost accounting, adverse-effect reporting, and independent evaluation.
- Do not market credits as proven improvements in well-being until measured.

### 2.5 Consensus is a security mechanism, not value creation

NIST IR 8202 describes blockchain as a distributed, tamper-evident ledger and compares consensus approaches. It describes proof-of-work as a computationally difficult barrier against invalid block publication, while identifying computational intensity, power consumption, and hardware arms races as disadvantages.

This supports a precise distinction:
- Hash work can secure ordering/consensus under a defined threat model.
- Hash work does not, by itself, prove useful human contribution or equivalent economic value.
- Proof-of-stake changes the resource used to secure consensus; it also does not prove social usefulness.
- NT XII should select consensus only after defining the trust model, adversary budget, finality requirement, governance, privacy, and operating cost.

### 2.6 Open-source systems worth evaluating, not blindly adopting

1. Automerge — https://github.com/automerge/automerge
   CRDT structures and synchronization for local-first applications. Strong candidate for offline collaboration and evidence drafts. Not a monetary settlement engine.

2. Holochain — https://github.com/holochain/holochain
   Agent-centric peer-to-peer application framework. Its repository describes the code as an initial beta release undergoing security review and protocol hardening. Candidate for prototyping distributed validation, but not a production recommendation without current security, maintenance, and deployment review.

3. Hyperledger Fabric — https://github.com/hyperledger/fabric
   Apache-2.0 permissioned distributed ledger with modular consensus and privacy controls. Candidate for a consortium settlement trial where member organizations jointly operate validators. Its permissioned governance is a trade-off, not permissionless decentralization.

4. Duniter — https://github.com/duniter/duniter
   Open-source software for libre currency and a web-of-trust / universal-dividend approach; Ğ1 is a live historical deployment. The GitHub organization notes migration and partially unmaintained repositories, with current development moved to GitLab. Candidate for comparative study of personhood-linked issuance, not an unqualified implementation dependency.

5. Ethereum Attestation Service — https://github.com/ethereum-attestation-service/eas-contracts
   MIT-licensed open protocol for schema-based attestations on EVM networks. Useful as a reference for attestations and evidence schemas. On-chain attestation does not make the underlying claim true.

6. GNU Taler — https://taler.net/
   Candidate for a payment-privacy and merchant-payment study. Its design goals and current deployment/maintenance status must be independently rechecked before any integration decision. It is not a contribution-verification system.

No candidate is selected as the NT XII mainnet. The design should remain protocol-neutral until a reproducible comparison shows why a substrate is necessary.

## 3. Core architecture proposed for NT XII

The correct starting point is a federated, offline-first reciprocal-value network with a portable evidence layer and pluggable settlement—not a speculative global coin contract.

### 3.1 Keep four instruments distinct

A single token should not conflate these instruments:

A. Contribution credential
A non-monetary, signed record that a contribution occurred. It records who attested, what was done, when, under which community rules, and what evidence supports it. It is not money and is not automatically redeemable.

B. Community credit
A non-transferable or locally transferable entitlement to access community-provided goods, services, or capacity. Its issuance is bounded by actual community supply and governance rules. It may be denominated in hours, service units, or a local unit of account, but its meaning must be explicit.

C. Mutual-credit balance
A double-entry claim and obligation between members of a defined network. Credit creation is bounded by approved limits, counterparty consent, and clearing rules. The unit is an accounting unit, not a promise of fiat redemption unless a separate reserve and legal obligation exist.

D. Redeemable monetary claim
A claim redeemable for a specified asset, service, or fiat amount. It requires explicit backing, custody, redemption rights, liquidity, insolvency treatment, consumer protection, and jurisdiction-specific legal review. Never imply this instrument exists merely because a token is transferable.

### 3.2 Evidence-to-value pipeline

Proposed serial pipeline:

1. Capture: contributor creates an offline event record with a unique event ID, timestamp quality, local sequence, and scope.
2. Consent: contributor chooses which evidence is shared and with whom; sensitive source data stays local or encrypted.
3. Attestation: one or more independent witnesses/recipients sign scoped claims.
4. Outcome: record the delivered result, quantity, quality, recipient acceptance, and any measurable externality.
5. Classification: identify contribution type (care, mentoring, maintenance, knowledge, ecological stewardship, production, governance, defect closure, etc.).
6. Valuation: apply a community-approved valuation rule, with units, provenance, uncertainty, and appeal path.
7. Reciprocity: record the reciprocal obligation or benefit allocation, not just the contributor's input.
8. Posting: create balanced debit/credit entries in an append-only journal.
9. Risk checks: duplicate, collusion, self-dealing, circular exchange, wash activity, replay, coercion, and cap checks.
10. Settlement: finalize only under the network's explicit consensus and double-spend rules.
11. Reconciliation: reconcile local replicas, external resource accounts, and community reserves.
12. Audit: publish privacy-safe proofs, aggregate impact, and the exact rule version used.

A rejected, disputed, unverified, or provisional event remains visible as such. It must not be silently converted into a PASS or mint.

### 3.3 Value cannot be reduced to energy expenditure

Record at least these separate dimensions:
- contributor time and resource cost;
- delivered quantity;
- verified quality;
- recipient acceptance;
- outcome durability;
- scarcity/availability of the supplied good or service;
- avoided harm or externality, if measured;
- community priority and democratic mandate;
- opportunity cost and accessibility;
- uncertainty and evidence strength.

Do not automatically convert calories, CPU cycles, attention minutes, or time spent into money. These are input measures. A monetary value requires a disclosed bridge, such as an agreed replacement cost, market reference, negotiated contract price, public budget rate, or a community-set non-cash exchange schedule. Where no defensible bridge exists, issue a credential or non-monetary credit, not a cash-equivalent token.

### 3.4 Dimensional closure and the NT XIII Bill

Before coding the Bill, freeze a notation registry:
- distinguish energy from external extraction;
- distinguish stock from flow;
- distinguish bits from bytes;
- distinguish local unit-of-account from fiat currency;
- state whether each term is a quantity, rate, stock, price, or ratio;
- define each bridge and its measurement protocol;
- prove each integrated term resolves to the same currency unit;
- add unit tests and compile-time value types for each dimension.

A Bill is an accounting output, not automatically a token mint. A positive residual can indicate an unreturned cost or extraction; it does not alone establish a receivable against a particular party. The recipient, contract, evidence, allocation rule, and enforceable settlement basis must be identified.

## 4. Offline-first architecture and the no-double-spend boundary

### 4.1 Local node

Each participant/community node should be able to:
- create signed event records offline;
- store evidence in an encrypted local store;
- maintain a durable outbox;
- display pending, disputed, accepted, and settled states distinctly;
- verify signatures and schemas offline;
- sync peer-to-peer when available;
- export the full ledger in an open, documented format;
- operate without a mandatory vendor account or always-on central API.

### 4.2 Replication

Use CRDTs for mergeable collaboration metadata and evidence collections only where the data type's merge semantics are proven. Use immutable signed events and deterministic reducers for accounting. Never resolve two spends by last-write-wins.

### 4.3 Offline monetary policy

A device partitioned from the network cannot know whether another disconnected device has already spent the same globally scarce balance. Therefore choose one explicit policy:
- provisional offline credits, finalized on reconciliation;
- pre-allocated offline spend envelopes with strict per-device caps and expiry;
- escrowed balances reserved before disconnection;
- local closed-loop credits redeemable only within a clearly bounded community;
- no offline final settlement.

Any design promising unrestricted offline transfers and immediate global finality without coordination is a no-go.

### 4.4 Ledger core

The economic core should be:
- append-only, event-sourced;
- double-entry balanced;
- cryptographically signed by authorized keys;
- hash-chained or Merkle-committed for tamper evidence;
- idempotent under replay;
- sequence-aware and causally linked;
- explicit about reversals and corrections (never delete history);
- deterministic across independent validators;
- exportable and independently recomputable;
- backed by reproducible audit tooling.

A hash proves integrity of bytes under assumptions. It does not prove that an event happened, that an attestor was honest, or that the contribution was valuable.

## 5. Trial network and adversarial proving ground

The trial network must be a separate trust domain, not a staging switch on the production ledger.

Mandatory separation:
- separate chain/database namespace, keys, identifiers, genesis state, endpoints, storage, and credentials;
- every test artifact carries environment=SIMULATION and a non-production issuer namespace;
- production verifiers reject simulation issuers and simulation evidence by default;
- no testnet balances can be converted to mainnet balances;
- no testnet private key or credential is accepted by production;
- synthetic data is clearly labeled and cannot be mistaken for real people or contributions;
- test fixtures may use mocks inside the test harness, but a mock can never satisfy a real integration gate.

Adversarial suite must include:
- duplicate identity / Sybil farms;
- bought or rented identities;
- colluding witnesses and reciprocal fake attestations;
- self-dealing and circular trades;
- fabricated, altered, replayed, backdated, and selectively omitted evidence;
- compromised contributor, witness, issuer, and validator keys;
- revoked and expired credentials;
- network partitions and clock skew;
- concurrent offline spends and double-spend attempts;
- lost devices and restored backups;
- malicious sync peers and corrupted CRDT payloads;
- validator capture, governance capture, censorship, and cartel behavior;
- reserve shortfall and redemption runs, if redeemable claims are ever considered;
- coercive labor, unpaid moderation, hidden operating costs, and unequal access;
- denial-of-service, spam, privacy correlation, metadata leakage, and deanonymization;
- protocol upgrade and schema migration failures;
- disaster recovery and independent ledger reconstruction.

A gate passes only when the adversarial test produces the expected rejection, preserves the evidence, and proves that no economic state was mutated. Testnet results must be reproducible from pinned fixtures and versioned test definitions.

## 6. Mainnet admission gates — no shortcuts

No mainnet or real-value issuance until all gates pass:

G1 — Formulary closure: every equation, symbol, unit, bridge, and conversion is dimensionally defined and independently reviewed.

G2 — Economic semantics: each instrument has a precise legal/economic meaning, issuance rule, cap, redemption/transfer policy, and loss allocation.

G3 — Evidence validity: contribution claims are independently attestable, recipient acceptance is recorded where relevant, and uncertainty/disputes are explicit.

G4 — Sybil/collusion resistance: the threat model and privacy trade-offs are documented; attack simulations meet published thresholds.

G5 — Accounting: double-entry invariant holds; all postings balance; reversals are explicit; independent replay reconstructs identical balances.

G6 — Offline safety: no unrestricted offline double spend; provisional and settled states cannot be confused.

G7 — Security: independent threat model, code review, cryptographic review, key recovery, revocation, incident response, and penetration testing.

G8 — Governance: one-member-one-vote or another explicitly justified rule; worker and community representation; recall; conflict-of-interest controls; no unilateral issuer privilege.

G9 — Anti-extraction: all platform fees, validator rewards, data use, labor, infrastructure, reserves, and administrative costs are disclosed. Contributors can export records and leave without losing legitimate claims.

G10 — Impact: preregistered measures of well-being, resource use, retention, distribution, and adverse effects; independent evaluation; costs reported.

G11 — Legal review: jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction assessment of payment services, e-money, virtual assets, money transmission, consumer protection, taxation, privacy, labor, cooperative, and securities rules.

G12 — Operational proof: deterministic rebuild, disaster recovery, ledger export, independent validator replay, and zero unresolved critical/high findings.

G13 — Human authorization: a real-value mainnet launch, token issuance, reserve movement, or irreversible contract deployment requires a separate explicit operator/governance authorization. Research permission is not deployment permission.

## 7. Governance and anti-capture requirements

Decentralization is not a property conferred by a blockchain label. Measure effective control across:
- software maintainers and release signing;
- validators and network operators;
- identity/credential issuers;
- evidence reviewers and dispute panels;
- treasury and reserve custody;
- protocol upgrade keys;
- infrastructure and hosting;
- data access and analytics;
- legal entities and jurisdictions.

For each component, disclose concentration, dependencies, veto rights, emergency powers, and exit options. Require multi-party control for high-impact actions, transparent budgets, independent audits, conflict-of-interest disclosure, community recall, and a public change log.

Do not use an automated Gini or carbon metric to seize assets or alter voting rights without a defined legal basis, measurement uncertainty model, due process, appeal, and human authorization. The AFL may recommend or execute bounded reversible adjustments approved in advance; it must not become an unaccountable sovereign.

## 8. Philippine jurisdiction checkpoint

The Philippines is the first operational context implied by the repository's Asia/Manila runtime, but it is not the only jurisdiction the system must eventually support.

BSP Circular 1206 (2024) broadly defines virtual assets to include digital units used as a medium of exchange or stored value, whether centrally administered, decentralized, or created through computing/manufacturing effort, while distinguishing certain closed-loop issuer/merchant and in-game units. Classification depends on actual function, not the project's preferred label.

BSP Memorandum M-2025-031 states that the moratorium on new VASP licenses continued from 1 September 2025, subject to reassessment. BSP Memorandum M-2026-003 reminds supervised financial institutions to conduct due diligence and enhanced due diligence where appropriate when dealing with VASPs. BSP's 2025 regulations also define and regulate e-money issuer activity, with a stated closed-loop exception in the cited section.

Implication: do not launch a public transferable token, custody service, exchange, fiat redemption, or payment rail in the Philippines on the assumption that calling it a community credit removes regulation. Obtain Philippine legal advice and direct regulator guidance before any real-value deployment. The regulatory position must be rechecked at launch; this report is not legal advice.

## 9. Initial architecture decision

Provisional recommendation for research, not a final implementation choice:

Layer 1 — Local-first participant/community application:
encrypted local database, signed event journal, durable outbox, open export, and accessible low-bandwidth UI.

Layer 2 — Evidence and credential layer:
W3C VC/DID-compatible claims where useful, scoped/pairwise identifiers, issuer trust registries, revocation, and privacy-preserving proofs.

Layer 3 — Reciprocal accounting:
deterministic event reducer, double-entry journal, dimensional types, explicit credit limits, and a community-controlled unit of account.

Layer 4 — Federation:
community nodes exchange signed events and clearing proposals; each community retains its own governance and ledger sovereignty. Inter-community clearing is negotiated, not silently imposed.

Layer 5 — Settlement adapter:
pluggable backends evaluated independently: mutual-credit clearing, permissioned consortium DLT, agent-centric P2P validation, or public-chain attestations. Do not select a public blockchain before the threat model and legal/economic instrument require one.

Layer 6 — Public audit and impact:
privacy-safe proofs, aggregate retention and extraction measures, distributional impact, independent reproduction, and published correction/dispute records.

The network should be useful before a cryptocurrency exists. If the community ledger cannot deliver useful accounting, reciprocal exchange, and exit portability without speculation, a token will only make the failure tradable.

## 11. Current evidence status

Established by standards or technical literature:
- offline-first local storage and synchronization patterns exist;
- CRDTs can provide formal convergence for suitable replicated data types;
- W3C VC 2.0 and DID Core 1.0 are published Recommendations;
- blockchains provide tamper-evident replicated ledgers under defined consensus assumptions;
- proof-of-work is a consensus barrier, not proof of useful human output;
- community/time currencies have documented examples but limited high-quality impact evidence.

Open-source prior art exists:
- Automerge, Holochain, Hyperledger Fabric, Duniter, EAS, and GNU Taler are candidates for scoped comparison.

Repository-grounded:
- NT XI contains an RLCR and ASE architecture.
- NT XII states a minted-value and closure doctrine.
- NT XIII contains an OFIF accounting formulation and acknowledges that much of its component prior art is not novel.
- The inspected minting method is not yet a verified economic issuance mechanism.
- The inspected progress ledger is not yet a globally settled, double-entry, offline-safe ledger.

Not established:
- that one universal token can represent all forms of human contribution fairly;
- that exhaustion or energy expenditure alone is transferable value;
- that a global proof-of-personhood scheme can simultaneously guarantee privacy, decentralization, uniqueness, and universal inclusion;
- that unconditional offline final settlement can prevent double spending;
- that the proposed ASE has already produced measured global reductions in extraction;
- that any candidate open-source substrate is secure or fit for NT XII mainnet without independent review.

## 14. Falsifiers and stop conditions

Stop the design if:
- the value equation cannot be made dimensionally closed;
- the token's redemption or exchange promise cannot be honored from named resources;
- independent reviewers cannot distinguish real evidence from synthetic or self-issued evidence;
- an offline partition permits duplicate final spending;
- governance gives one operator unilateral control over issuance, evidence acceptance, upgrades, and treasury;
- privacy protections fail under correlation analysis;
- measured community outcomes do not justify costs or worsen the position of the least powerful participants;
- the legal classification is unresolved for the intended activity;
- any testnet artifact can cross into mainnet as an accepted contribution or balance;
- the production gate can pass while a required mechanism is mocked, skipped, or unimplemented.

The system earns the right to issue a token only after it proves the reciprocal exchange that the token is supposed to represent.
